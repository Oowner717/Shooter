Seven Bands of Seven a rebalance to level 49

A boss every seventh level, seven bands, and a hard stop at 49. Enemy pressure arrives as more bodies rather than tougher ones, the stream never stops, and the turret is meant to be winning almost all of the time.

What is wrong, measured

Those four numbers are the whole brief. The ladder asks the turret to cover a two-hundred-fold increase in what arrives while selling it a four-fold increase in what it can do; it runs out of authored waves at rung nine and pads the remaining forty rungs with health; and because every node in the tree costs the same, there is no order to buy them in and no such thing as an early upgrade or a late one.

The sawtooth

This is the shape everything else serves. Difficulty is not a curve the player chases; it is seven steps that a rising ramp swallows seven times. The feeling asked for — constant progress, numbers going up, minimal difficulty — is what that looks like from the inside: you are always winning, and the only question is by how much. The margin is never allowed below about ×1.4, because a player who is losing has to grind, and never above about ×7, because a player who cannot lose stops buying. Both ends of that range are assertions rather than hopes; see what proves it.

The spine: seven bands, seven levels each

Every seventh level is a boss and nothing else is. A band is the six ordinary levels plus the boss that closes it, and every quantity in this plan — flow, health, salvage, upgrade price, which objects appear — is authored per band rather than per level. Bands are the unit because bosses are, and because a seven-level block is the smallest thing a player can feel the shape of.

The boss slot table is data, not code. One list maps level to anomaly: 7 → ordinal, 14 → gnomon, 21 → fractal, 28 → amplitude, 35 → dynamo, 42 → parity, 49 → terminus. A slot with no anomaly in it plays a DRIFT SURGE instead: a full level of nothing but grey, worth real salvage, no threat, and a visible banner saying the aperture is dark. That is what makes adding the eighth and ninth bosses a one-line change, and it is also the honest answer for any slot whose boss is pulled or unfinished.

One decision this plan cannot make for you

There are nine anomalies built and only seven slots at or below 49. AXIOM and TESSERA have nowhere to go. The default here is that they take slots 56 and 63, dark until the ceiling moves — and that they are reachable in the meantime through the aperture as optional trials, which Director.arm already supports. The alternative is bands of six, which fits eight bosses by 48 and still leaves one over. Seven is what you asked for, so seven is what this is built on.

Quantity, not health

Quantity has a hard ceiling and throughput does not. CFG.maxEnemies is 57 and a phone has a frame budget, so "more enemies" cannot mean more on the screen at once past about forty. It means more arriving per second: the field holds a roughly constant crowd while the stream through it gets faster and the wave gets longer. Band 1 releases a body every 1.11s and puts about seven on the field; band 7 releases one every 0.20s and holds about forty. The wave goes from forty-five bodies to nine hundred and fifty.

Health does move, just gently: hpStep 1.085 → 1.028 a level, which is ×3.8 by level 49 instead of ×50. Salvage per body rises faster than health does, so a body is always worth more than it costs to kill, and the purse climbs with the stream rather than with the clock.

The wave engine, rebuilt

The present engine authors 38 waves by hand, runs out of them at rung 9, and separates every wave from the next with a 2.6–4.2s rest. Four changes make it a stream instead.

What does not change

The verdict rules stay exactly as they are: a wave is judged on its own bodies, surge climbs two rungs, clean climbs one, stall holds, and the glitch discharge is still the only involuntary way down. Build 291's release gate stays too — a wave still waits for the field to be as thin as the last one was required to leave it — because without it the faster stream compounds leftovers, which is the bug that gate exists for. What changes is that the gate is rarely the thing that is waiting, because the turret is meant to be ahead.

Where the twenty objects arrive

The twenty in the object guide, placed by what they teach rather than by what they weigh. Two or three a band, each one arriving in the band whose flow makes its gait legible: SHOAL needs a stream thick enough for a school to read as a school, and ANVIL needs a gun big enough that twenty-six seconds of walking is a decision rather than a wall.

Three of them are load-bearing for other parts of this plan and should not be moved. CHAFF (band 4) is the first object that makes the assist worse than a thumb, which is what earns DEEP ARRAY and OPEN SIEVE. LANTERN (band 4) is the first object that costs you something for ignoring it, which is what teaches that the field has a clock. ANVIL (band 7) is the one body that refuses every impulse in the game, which is what makes the last band about damage.

The turret has to reach twenty-one

Incoming strength scales ×21 from band 1 to band 7. The whole tree is currently worth ×4.45 on BOLT damage a second — the damage line is exactly ×4.00 and cadence is ×1.11 — so the tree has to become about five times deeper without adding a single cadence node, because cadence has been removed three times for good reasons and reopening it is how this game gets a nineteen-fold damage line by accident.

Two changes, both multiplicative, both on ALL ROUNDS. HOLLOWPOINT goes from five levels at 1.32 to eight at 1.26 — the same shape as build 229's change, arriving further up the ladder — and a new node, CORE, sits dormant until RECAST is owned and then sells four levels at 1.35. That is ×6.35 from the old line and ×3.32 from the new one: ×21.1 on damage, ×30.4 once cadence and era2Power are counted. The turret ends the run comfortably ahead, which is the brief.

CORE is where the overpowered window comes from. RECAST is offered when the fourth anomaly is reconciled, at level 28; the four levels of CORE are priced for band 5 and affordable inside it; and the boss at 35 is the first thing authored to survive them. So levels 29 to 34 are played by a turret that has just tripled while the field has not moved — and the guard is not a nerf, it is the next step of the staircase.

The glitch, and why automation is the answer to it

With difficulty this gentle, the discharge is the only hazard left in the game — and it is the right one, because it is the only mechanism that can take a level away. The plan keeps it exactly as punishing as it is (one rung, a field dissolve, a shake) and changes only how often the player meets it.

  • Early, it teaches. Bands 1 and 2 have a thin enough stream that a grab is an event. The fuse fills, the ring shows, the caption names the cause, and nothing is lost the first time — the first discharge of a run is free, a step-back that is announced and then refunded, once.
  • Late, it is structural. The crowd term is half the contact rate, so a band-7 field of forty bodies fills the fuse on its own without anything ever reaching the mount. At that flow a thumb cannot keep the mount clear, and the fuse is the scoreboard for that.
  • The answer is already in the tree. FLINCH fires PULSE when something takes hold; DEADBOLT stands a WARD. Measured at build 291, the pair took the mount from 22.3% gripped to 10.2% and discharges from six to one over seven minutes. They are the two nodes that convert "keep clearing the mount yourself" into "the machine does it", and this plan prices them as the cheapest nodes in bands 3 and 4 — the first real automation a player can afford, arriving in the band where the fuse starts filling on its own.

Manual play should become obsolete on purpose. The ladder from here is AUTO AIM, then DEEP ARRAY and OPEN SIEVE so it covers the field and sorts grey from hostile, then SLEW so the barrel keeps up with a target switch, then STANDING ORDER so every clock on the bar runs a third faster, then FLINCH and DEADBOLT so the mount clears itself. Nine of the tree's 107 levels are automation; they should be the nine a player most wants, and a band-7 field is the argument for them. Nothing is taken away — a thumb still works, and still out-aims the assist against CHAFF — but by band 6 the run should be watchable rather than played.

What things cost

Today every node is priced 500 kB + 350 kB a level, whatever it does and whenever it is meant to be bought. The replacement is one formula and one number per band: a node's price is set by the band it is meant for, and each level of it costs 60% more than the last.

That tripling per band is what makes early upgrades feel free and late ones feel earned, and it is what makes "which band is this node for?" a real authoring decision — the thing a flat price cannot express. The whole tree comes to about 116 MB across the same 107 levels, against 107.5 MB today, so the totals are close; what changes is the distribution. Two thirds of the spend is in the last two bands.

Income follows the stream rather than the clock, so it needs no separate curve: more bodies arriving is more salvage per second by construction. bountyStep comes down from 1.075 to 1.045 a level (×8.4 by 49 instead of ×32) precisely because quantity is now doing that work, and the two together give the income column in the table above.

The bosses

Boss health is authored per slot, not scaled by tier. Each anomaly is met exactly once, at a known level, against a turret whose damage at that level is known from the table above — so there is no reason to run it through scaleToTier and every reason not to. The target is a fight between seventy seconds and just under three minutes, with about 62% of the window spent on the boss itself and the rest on what it puts on the field.

The seventh slot is the ceiling, and TERMINUS should be the hardest thing in the simulation by a clear margin: 358k of core and structure against a fully bought era-2 turret. It is the only fight in the plan that is allowed to be genuinely difficult, because it is the last one.

The patience clock needs a look

CFG.boss.patience is 150 seconds and two of these targets are at or past it. A boss that withdraws because the clock ran out reads as a bug when the fight was going fine — and build 279 found TESSERA withdrawing at 164s with its core untouched. Patience should be authored per slot alongside health, at about 2.2× the target.

The ceiling at 49

Level 49 is the end. The ladder refuses to climb past it, the verdict still lands and still pays, and the run continues at 49 for as long as the player wants — the stream does not stop, salvage does not stop, and the tree can still be finished. What stops is the number.

Three things this has to get right. It has to arrive once, on the verdict that would have moved 49 to 50, and not on every wave after it — the same once-per-target guard the rail's step-back mark already uses. It has to be a statement rather than a failure: the run is not over, and the copy says so in its second line. And the rail has to show it — rung 49 drawn as a closed end rather than as a rung with nothing after it, so the ceiling is legible before it is reached.

Mechanically it is the era ceiling that already exists. eraHeld holds the ladder at rung 42 until NEW FORM is owned; the same shape, at 49, with nothing that lifts it. Both doors have to carry it — climbTo refuses the climb and setTier is the machinery's setter and does not gate, which is how build 272 walked straight over its own ceiling.

Order of work

What proves it

  • The flow, per band, off the real director. Arrivals a second, standing field, bodies per wave and wave length, sampled at levels 4, 11, 18, 25, 32, 39 and 46. The claim is the table in this plan; the probe is tiers.mjs extended to report arrivals rather than only clears.
  • The sawtooth, as a margin. For each band, the turret's damage a second divided by the field's incoming health a second, with only the purchases that band's income affords. The margin must stay inside 1.4 to 7.0 at every level from 1 to 49 — measured at the start of a band, which is its worst point, and at the level before its boss, which is its best. Outside that range in either direction is a failure: below it the player grinds, above it they stop buying.
  • The ceiling, in both doors. A case that climbs to 49 through climbTo and is refused, and a second that tries setTier(50) and is refused, and a third asserting the message paints once across twenty further waves.
  • The glitch, per band. Peak fuse and discharge count over seven minutes at bands 2, 5 and 7, with and without FLINCH and DEADBOLT. The claim is that the pair is the difference between a fuse that blows and one that does not at band 7 — and record the peak beside the count, because 0.87 of a fuse and 0.2 of a fuse are both zero discharges.
  • The economy, end to end. One headless run to 49 recording earnings and the affordable purchase set at every rung. The assertion is that the tree finishes between 55% and 70% of run earnings, and that no band's purchase set costs more than that band pays.
  • The ORDINAL hash will move, and by a lot. Every number in this plan is in the mix — payout, positions, what the turret does. Take it on HEAD first, expect the move, record the cause. It stops being a regression instrument for the duration and becomes one again on the first build after the numbers settle.

Left open

  • AXIOM and TESSERA. Above the ceiling by default. See the note under the spine.
  • Trait pressure. traitFrom 10 and traitPair 25 put ARMORED, SWARM, MENDING, TETHERED and EBB on bodies from rung 10 and pairs from 25. Under the new scaling those rungs are mid-band-2 and mid-band-4; they probably want to be band boundaries, but traits interact with the twenty new objects in ways worth measuring before deciding.
  • Era 2's field. RECAST moves from level 42 to 28, which means the wider field, the wall and the yard arrive fourteen rungs earlier than they do now. The wall changes what the assist can reach and the field is 1.22× deeper; band 5's flow numbers here assume the era-1 field and will need re-measuring on the era-2 one.
  • Wave templates versus the budget. This plan fills waves from a strength budget. The 38 authored waves become templates for the teach band and for named set-pieces; which of them survive as set-pieces is a taste call nobody should make before the budget filler exists.
  • Overclock and Recall. Both are rung-10 nodes priced for a flat tree. Under the new curve they are band-2 nodes, and OVERCLOCK's double pay against a stream that is already continuous may be worth more than it was. Unmeasured.
